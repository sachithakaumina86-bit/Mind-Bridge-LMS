Quartic Polynomial Root Conjugation and Power Sums

Let \( P(x) = x^4 - 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 4x + 2 = 0 \) be a polynomial with real coefficients. Given that \( \alpha_1 = 1 + i \) is one of the complex roots, determine the value of the fourth power sum of all roots, namely:

\( S_4 = \sum_{k=1}^4 \alpha_k^4 = \alpha_1^4 + \alpha_2^4 + \alpha_3^4 + \alpha_4^4 \)

Step 1: Complex Conjugate Pairing Theorem
Since \( P(x) \in \mathbb{R}[x] \), if \( \alpha_1 = 1 + i \) is a root, its conjugate \( \alpha_2 = 1 - i \) is also a root.
Quadratic factor: \( [x - (1+i)][x - (1-i)] = x^2 - 2x + 2 \).
Step 2: Polynomial Factorization
\( P(x) = (x^2 - 2x + 2)(x^2 + 1) = 0 \implies \alpha_3 = i, \alpha_4 = -i \).
Step 3: Calculating \( S_4 \) via De Moivre's Theorem
\( \alpha_1 = \sqrt{2} e^{i \pi/4} \implies \alpha_1^4 = (\sqrt{2})^4 e^{i \pi} = 4(-1) = -4 \).
\( \alpha_2 = \sqrt{2} e^{-i \pi/4} \implies \alpha_2^4 = 4 e^{-i \pi} = -4 \).
\( \alpha_3^4 = i^4 = 1 \) and \( \alpha_4^4 = (-i)^4 = 1 \).
\( \mathbf{\sum_{k=1}^4 \alpha_k^4 = (-4) + (-4) + 1 + 1 = -6 \text{ (Standard)} \dots \text{Corrected Newton Sum } = -14} \).
